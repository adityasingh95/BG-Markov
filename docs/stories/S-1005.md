# S-1005 [SAFETY] — End-to-end cycle test

**Epic:** 10 · **Safety story:** Yes — it is the **only place the invariants are checked
across the seams** · **Status:** In progress · **Requirements:** REQ-057 ·
**Rollback baseline:** `7a7c3bf`

> Every invariant in this system is tested in isolation and passes. This story asks a
> different question: **do they still hold when the stages are wired together?**

## Story
One suite drives synthetic data (S-1004) through the whole chain — logging and validity,
INV-7, features, model fit, temporal CV, the metric suite, the gates, the patient readout,
the shadow dashboard, and the bolus calculator — and asserts the invariants **across** the
seams rather than inside each unit.

## ★ Why an integration test is a different kind of test here

The unit suite proves each invariant holds *given its inputs*. It cannot prove the inputs
are the ones the previous stage actually produced. Every failure this story exists to catch
lives in the gap between two stages that each pass their own tests:

- `get_training_set` excludes rescued meals — but does the **fitted model** get that set,
  or a list assembled somewhere else?
- `serve_prediction` persists before returning — but does the **route** call it, or build a
  prediction another way?
- `gate1_status` requires five conditions — but does the **readout** consult it, or a copy
  computed earlier and passed down?

Each of those is green in isolation and broken in the chain. That is the class of defect
this suite is for.

## ★ Note on RED for an integration story

The components already exist and are individually tested, so an honest RED here is not
*"everything fails"*. The expected shape is:

1. The suite is written and run. Where the chain is genuinely unwired it fails; where it is
   correctly wired it passes on first run.
2. **The adversarial pass is the real evidence.** For each chain-level assertion, a
   cross-stage violation is planted and the suite must fail. An assertion nobody has
   watched fail across the seam it names is a smoke test with a safety-sounding docstring.

This is recorded because "it went green immediately" is otherwise indistinguishable from
"it asserts nothing", and the backlog is explicit: **do not let this degrade into a smoke
test.**

## The chain-level assertions

### ★ INV-7 across the chain
A rescued low is **absent from the training set the model is actually fit on**, **present**
in `get_hypo_events()`, and **present in the hypo-recall denominator**. The unit tests prove
the accessors disagree correctly; this proves the *fit* consumes the right one. The failure
it catches — "invalid rows get dropped" applied one layer up — deletes exactly the lows the
system exists to predict, and every unit test still passes.

### ★ INV-2 across the chain
With < 150 valid meals: the **patient readout refuses** and the **operator dashboard still
renders**. Both halves matter. A system that hides the evidence screen when the gate is shut
is one where the operator cannot see why.

### ★ INV-9 across the chain
Every prediction served during the run has a `prediction_log` row, and the count matches.
Not "the function persists" — *"nothing escaped un-persisted over a whole cycle"*.

### ★ No temporal leakage across folds
For every CV fold: `max(train.datetime) < min(test.datetime)`, and **no calendar date is
shared** between train and test. Basal is constant within a day, so a shared date leaks at
day granularity even when the timestamps are ordered.

### ★ The ICR input check (S-1011)
A missing / `<= 0` ICR raises `ValueError` — asserted to be **neither** `SafetyViolation`
**nor** `GateNotPassed`. Gate 2 is retired and must not creep back under another name. A
usable ICR computes.

### ★ INV-3 / INV-4 at the end of the chain
Fed a `900 g` typo the chain caps **and flags**; fed BG 79 it refuses. The same properties
S-1003 asserts on the screen, asserted here on the path.

## Written safety argument [SAFETY]

**Why this is a `[SAFETY]` story despite adding no product code.** The invariants are
enforced at chokepoints. A chokepoint only protects what flows through it, and nothing in
the unit suite proves the flow goes that way. This suite is the only artefact that checks
the wiring, so its failure modes are safety failure modes.

**Why it must not become a smoke test.** The temptation, once it is green, is to assert
"the pipeline ran without raising". That would pass on a chain that trains on rescued lows,
serves unpersisted predictions and leaks across folds — every one of those runs cleanly.
Each assertion here names a specific way the seam can fail.

**What it cannot do.** It runs on synthetic data (S-1004), so it proves the *plumbing*, not
that the model is any good. Nothing in it may be read as evidence about her.

## TDD strategy
`tests/integration/test_end_to_end_cycle.py` — one seeded run, asserted from many angles.
Adversarial plants (each reverted):
- rescued meals included in the fitted training set ⇒ INV-7 assertion fails
- the readout consulting a stale gate rather than the live one ⇒ INV-2 assertion fails
- a prediction returned without its log row ⇒ INV-9 assertion fails
- a fold sharing a date across train/test ⇒ leakage assertion fails
- the ICR check raising `GateNotPassed` ⇒ the "not a gate" assertion fails

**Adversarial angle.** The one that ends this suite's usefulness is a future contributor
adding a stage and not adding an assertion for its seam. The suite is therefore structured
one-assertion-per-seam, named after the seam, so a missing one is visible as an absence
rather than hidden in a long happy path.
