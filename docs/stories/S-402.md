# S-402 — Effective basal (Tresiba / degludec)

**Epic:** 4 (Derived Features) · **Safety story:** No (but a safety-relevant
forbidden pattern) · **Status:** In progress · **Requirements:** REQ-021

> **Today's basal dose is not today's basal effect.** Degludec (Tresiba) has ~42 h
> duration and reaches steady state only after 3–4 days (`07` §3). Using the raw
> daily dose as a feature would mis-attribute a dose change to the day it was made,
> when its effect actually ramps over days. This is a named forbidden pattern.

## Story
Derive the **effective** background insulin as an EWMA of the daily basal series
(half-life 25 h), and flag the **titration lockout** — the 3 days after any dose
change, during which the basal coefficient is uninterpretable and basal-related
guidance must be suppressed.

## Requirement source
- **REQ-021** — effective basal is an EWMA of the daily dose with **half-life
  25 h**, not the raw dose; multi-day carryover is modelled.
- **`07` §3** — `effective_basal = basal_series.ewm(halflife="25h",
  times=timestamps).mean()`; titration lockout of 3 days after a change, guidance
  suppressed during it.
- **CLAUDE.md forbidden pattern** — "Today's basal dose as a feature → EWMA,
  halflife 25 h."

## Design
- **`features/basal.py`**:
  - `BASAL_HALFLIFE = "25h"`, `TITRATION_LOCKOUT_DAYS = 3`.
  - `effective_basal(doses)` — `doses` is `[(datetime, units), …]` (one per day,
    ascending). Returns the EWMA-smoothed effective dose aligned to each dose,
    using `pandas` `ewm(halflife="25h", times=…).mean()` so the decay is in real
    time, not row count.
  - `in_titration_lockout(doses, at)` — `True` if `at` falls in the window
    `[C, C + 3 days]` of any dose **change** (a dose differing from the immediately
    preceding one). Erring toward suppression: the change day through +3 days
    inclusive are locked; +4 days is clear.
  - Pure functions; no clock read, no DB (the caller supplies the series, typically
    from `basal_log`).

## Safety note
The lockout exists so a basal coefficient learnt during the ramp is never trusted:
during `[C, C+3d]`, basal-related guidance is suppressed. This is guidance
suppression, not a gate — but it protects against acting on an uninterpretable
signal. A too-narrow lockout would trust a coefficient mid-ramp; the window errs
wide (includes the change day) on purpose.

## TDD strategy (SDET, written first — seen to fail)
`tests/unit/test_basal.py`:
- **★ Multi-day carryover:** a step change 24 → 30 U. At **+1 day** the effective
  basal is **strictly between** 24 and 30 (not yet 30 — the dose has not taken full
  effect). At **+5 days** it is **within 0.5 of 30** (steady state reached). This is
  the test that asserts the carryover is actually modelled, not a step.
- **No spurious carryover on a flat series:** a constant 24 U series returns 24 at
  every point (within tolerance).
- **Lockout window:** a change on day D ⇒ `in_titration_lockout` is `True` at
  **+1, +2, +3 days** and `False` at **+4 days**; `True` on the change day itself
  (suppress from the change).
- **No change ⇒ never locked:** a constant series is never in lockout.
- **Half-life constant is 25 h** (guards the forbidden-pattern value).

**RED evidence:** `features/basal.py` does not exist ⇒ import error before impl.

**Adversarial angle:** the forbidden shortcut is `effective_basal = today's dose` —
which the step-change test kills directly: at +1 day it would read 30, but the real
effective dose is still ramping (~28). The EWMA with a real-time half-life is the
only thing that passes both the +1-day "strictly between" and +5-day "at steady
state" assertions.
