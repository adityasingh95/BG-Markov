# S-702 — Metric suite

**Epic:** 7 (Validation) · **Safety story:** No (but the metric *choice* is a safety
decision) · **Status:** In progress · **Requirements:** REQ-035

> **The metric you report decides what "good" means, and the wrong metric hides the one
> failure that matters.** On this imbalanced 5-class problem, predicting "State 3" every
> time scores ~65% plain accuracy while never once catching a low — a model that is
> useless in exactly the way that endangers her, wearing a good grade. So plain accuracy
> is **not reported at all**. The headline is **hypo recall at a fixed false-alarm
> rate**: of the lows that happened, how many did we flag, holding nuisance alarms
> constant.

## Story
Provide the reporting metric suite over out-of-sample predictions (from S-701's temporal
CV): **hypo recall @ fixed FAR (primary)**, Brier score, a reliability/calibration curve,
MAE on the continuous baseline, the Clarke error grid, and off-by-one-state rate. Plain
accuracy is not a function in the module, and `accuracy_score` appears nowhere (forbidden
guard).

## Design
`models/metrics.py` (the reporting module — pure; imports only `numpy`):

- **`hypo_recall_at_far(hypo_score, is_hypo, *, target_far=0.10) -> HypoRecallResult`**
  (PRIMARY). Pick the score threshold that **maximises hypo recall subject to
  `FAR ≤ target_far`** (FAR = false positives / non-hypo). Returns the recall, the
  achieved FAR, and the threshold. This is the number the gate (S-703) and promotion
  decisions hang on.
- **`brier_score(proba, y_true, states) -> float`** — multiclass Brier: mean over rows of
  `Σ_k (p_k − 1[y=k])²`, columns ordered by `states`.
- **`reliability_curve(prob, is_event, *, n_bins=10) -> tuple[CalibrationBin, …]`** — per
  non-empty bin, mean predicted probability vs observed frequency and count (the
  reliability diagram's data; "if it says 20%, it must happen 20% of the time").
- **`mae_mgdl(predicted_bg, true_bg) -> float`** — MAE on the continuous baseline.
- **`clarke_zone(reference_bg, predicted_bg) -> str`** (A–E) and
  **`clarke_grid(reference, predicted) -> dict[str, int]`** — the field-standard grid
  that weights error by **clinical danger**, not magnitude. The dangerous zones are D
  (**failure to detect** — truly low, predicted normal) and E (erroneous treatment).
- **`off_by_one_rate(pred_state, true_state) -> float`** and
  **`severe_state_error_rate(pred_state, true_state) -> float`** (|Δstate| ≥ 2 — the
  catastrophic ordinal errors). **No plain accuracy / exact-match rate** — that is the
  meaningless metric the spec forbids.

**Dataclasses:** `HypoRecallResult(recall, far, threshold, target_far)`,
`CalibrationBin(mean_predicted, observed_frequency, count)`.

## Why these and not accuracy [rationale]
Accuracy rewards predicting the majority state. The whole product is a probability about
a rare, dangerous event, so the suite is built from **recall of the rare event at
controlled cost** (hypo recall @ FAR), **whether the probabilities are honest**
(calibration, Brier), and **how clinically dangerous the errors are** (Clarke D/E,
severe-state-error) — never how often the majority guess happens to be right.

## TDD strategy (SDET, written first — seen to fail)
`tests/unit/test_metrics.py`:
- **★ Hypo recall @ FAR (primary).** On a constructed score/truth set, the chosen
  threshold has `FAR ≤ target`, and the reported recall equals the hand-computed recall
  at that threshold. A degenerate "flag nothing" model scores recall 0; a perfect score
  scores recall 1 at FAR 0.
- **Brier golden.** A hand-computed multiclass Brier on a tiny set matches to 1e-9;
  perfect one-hot predictions score 0.
- **Reliability.** Perfectly-calibrated synthetic probabilities produce bins whose mean
  predicted ≈ observed frequency.
- **MAE golden.** Hand-computed MAE on 5 pairs, exact.
- **★ Clarke golden — published/reference pairs land in documented zones**, including the
  two clinically critical **D** cases: truly-low-predicted-normal `(50,120)` and
  truly-high-predicted-normal `(300,150)`; the **E** reversals `(50,200)`,`(250,60)`;
  exact and within-20% land in **A**.
- **Off-by-one / severe.** Off-by-one rate and severe (|Δ|≥2) rate match hand counts.
- **No accuracy.** `accuracy_score` and a plain `accuracy`/`exact_match` function are
  absent from the module (the forbidden AST guard already enforces `accuracy_score`
  tree-wide; re-checked here for the reporting module).

**RED evidence:** `models/metrics.py` does not exist ⇒ import error before impl.

**Adversarial angle:** the trap is to reach for `accuracy_score` because it is the
one-line answer, and to report a fat number that means nothing here. The primary-metric
test and the Clarke-D goldens are the tripwires: they reward catching the low and weight
the "predicted normal while truly low" error as dangerous, so a majority-class model
cannot pass by looking accurate.
