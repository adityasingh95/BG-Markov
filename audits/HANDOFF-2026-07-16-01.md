### N1 — Arm the `now()`→clinical-timestamp guard for `bg_after_time` (major)
- **Do:** Add `"bg_after_time"` to `_CLINICAL_TS`. S-306 introduced a new **reported**
  clinical timestamp (`correction_event.bg_after_time`, the +4 h reading time) but did
  not register it, so the single most important forbidden-pattern guard is blind to it.
  I demonstrated the miss:
  `detect_datetime_now_on_clinical_ts(ast.parse("event.bg_after_time = datetime.now()"))`
  returns `[]` (no violation), while `post_bg_time` is correctly caught. Also add a
  positive assertion for `bg_after_time` alongside the existing targets in the guard's
  own test, and **strongly consider** a meta-test: every ORM column commented `# REPORTED`
  in `data/tables.py` must appear in `_CLINICAL_TS` — so the next reported timestamp
  cannot be added without arming the guard.
- **Where:** `tests/forbidden/test_forbidden_patterns.py:27-29` (`_CLINICAL_TS`) and the
  guard test at `:258-262`; the field is `data/tables.py:188`.
- **Why (for her):** the shipped code is correct today, but `bg_after_time` closes out a
  correction event that feeds ISF — "the most dangerous number." The natural wrong
  refactor (`bg_after_time = datetime.now()`) would fabricate it with no error and no
  warning; the guard exists to make exactly that *loud*.
- **Done when:** `bg_after_time` is in `_CLINICAL_TS`; the guard test asserts a firing
  hit for `CorrectionEvent(bg_after_time=datetime.now())` and `event.bg_after_time =
  datetime.now()`; and (if added) the `# REPORTED`↔`_CLINICAL_TS` meta-test is green.
